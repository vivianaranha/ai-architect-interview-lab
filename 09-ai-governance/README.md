# AI Governance

Use these for study and mock interviews. For every answer, add trade-offs, failure modes, and metrics.

## 1. What is AI governance?

**Model answer:** Policies, roles, controls, evidence, and oversight ensuring AI is lawful, safe, reliable, secure, and aligned to business objectives.

**Follow-up:** What trade-offs change this answer? What can fail in production? What would you measure?

## 2. What belongs in an AI inventory?

**Model answer:** Purpose, owner, models, data, vendors, users, risk class, integrations, regions, controls, evaluations, and lifecycle status.

**Follow-up:** What trade-offs change this answer? What can fail in production? What would you measure?

## 3. What is a model card?

**Model answer:** Documentation of model purpose, intended use, limitations, evaluation context, performance, and risk.

**Follow-up:** What trade-offs change this answer? What can fail in production? What would you measure?

## 4. What is a system card?

**Model answer:** Documentation for the complete AI solution including workflow, models, data, integrations, controls, users, risks, and evaluations.

**Follow-up:** What trade-offs change this answer? What can fail in production? What would you measure?

## 5. How do approval gates work?

**Model answer:** Risk-triggered checkpoints block downstream action until an authorized reviewer records a decision and evidence.

**Follow-up:** What trade-offs change this answer? What can fail in production? What would you measure?

## 6. What is AI risk tiering?

**Model answer:** Classifying systems by impact/risk so governance controls scale proportionally.

**Follow-up:** What trade-offs change this answer? What can fail in production? What would you measure?

## 7. Why is governance an architecture concern?

**Model answer:** Identity, logging, retention, evaluation, approvals, and auditability require technical design, not only policy documents.

**Follow-up:** What trade-offs change this answer? What can fail in production? What would you measure?

## 8. What evidence should be retained?

**Model answer:** Requirements, assessments, lineage, model versions, tests, evaluations, approvals, incidents, access logs, changes, and control results.

**Follow-up:** What trade-offs change this answer? What can fail in production? What would you measure?

## 9. How do you govern third-party AI?

**Model answer:** Assess privacy, security, residency, data use, contractual terms, reliability, model-change policy, auditability, and exit strategy.

**Follow-up:** What trade-offs change this answer? What can fail in production? What would you measure?

## 10. What is responsible AI architecturally?

**Model answer:** Embedding safety, privacy, fairness where relevant, transparency, accountability, human oversight, and measurable controls into the design.

**Follow-up:** What trade-offs change this answer? What can fail in production? What would you measure?
